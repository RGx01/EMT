🩺 The health strip

Along the top, your data at a glance — each figure a link:

  • Import bill and Export bill — how many half-hours need a look, or all clear (with Octopus connected).
  • Missing — half-hours with no record at all, and in how many gaps.
  • Last backup — how long ago, and what made it.
  • Database — its size.

While a job is rewriting history (an import, a gap fill, a re-fetch) the counts wait until it finishes.

🔥 The data heatmap

The same month-by-month heatmap as on Charts, with views of the data's state as well as its energy:

  • Import bill — is each half-hour's cost final?
  • Export bill — the same for export, which is final once its kWh settles.
  • Missing — without Octopus, the one data view: is every half-hour recorded?

A missing half-hour is {{ sw('missing') }}black and hatched on every view. The key under the heatmap's title gives each colour; Fixing the heatmap below says what each means and what to do about it. Issues only folds away the days with nothing to look at; a fold opens with a click.

🛠 The tools on a selection

Click a half-hour for its details. Drag across the heatmap (or click one cell and shift-click another) to select a span, then right-click it (a long press on a phone) for what can be done to it:

  • Re-fetch from Octopus — ask Octopus again for the span's kWh and cost, see what would change, and apply it or not.
  • Fill this gap — where the span holds missing half-hours: from Octopus, from a CSV (EMT gives you a template for the gap), or from your Octopus PDF bills.
  • Fill the missing export from PDF bills or a CSV.
  • Delete — the whole half-hours, import, export and the devices with them. Home Assistant's own readings are kept, so when the half-hours are filled again the devices are rebuilt from them.
  • Make a support file for the span.
Every change that cannot be undone takes a backup first.
🧭 Fixing the heatmap

Click a half-hour for its details: they say which of these it is. Right-click it, or a selection, for the tools.

Import bill

  • {{ sw('green') }}Final — Octopus's cost, or on a published-rate tariff the settled kWh × its rate. Nothing to do.
  • {{ sw('teal') }}Octopus's cost, EMT's House/EV split — the cost is final; the car's share of it is EMT's own estimate. Nothing to do.
  • {{ sw('lamber') }}Settled kWh, Octopus's cost not in yet — priced kWh × rate until it comes. Wait: EMT asks for it.
  • {{ sw('amber') }}kWh not settled yet — Octopus usually settles it within a day or two. Wait.
  • {{ sw('red') }}Needs a look — one of:
    • Not final after 7 days: Octopus has not given its cost yet. EMT keeps asking — hourly for two days, then daily, for 30 days. Re-fetch from Octopus asks now.
    • Octopus's figures disagree: its cost is not its kWh × its rate, it priced a different kWh from the one that settled, or its VAT is not the day's rate. These are Octopus's figures, outside EMT's control. When Octopus corrects them, a re-fetch takes the correction.
    • A completed dispatch with no House/EV split: Octopus's breakdown puts none of the half-hour on the car, though the car charged in it. A re-fetch takes a corrected breakdown once Octopus has one.
    • The devices cost more than the meter: the device breakdown reads too high and the house's too low (the bill itself is right). A re-fetch prices the devices again and clears it.
  • {{ sw('abandoned') }}No cost from Octopus, no longer asked — asked for 30 days and never answered, so EMT has stopped asking. The cost shown is kWh × rate: an estimate, not your bill. A re-fetch may still get it, but only Octopus can supply it. Some accounts have long stretches like this — whole days Octopus has never costed.
  • {{ sw('blue') }}Your correction — a rate you set. On an Intelligent tariff with Octopus connected, a re-fetch replaces it with Octopus's own cost.
  • {{ sw('grey') }}Nothing to settle — nothing imported, imported history, or not on an Octopus agreement.
  • {{ sw('missing') }}Missing — no record for the half-hour. Fill this gap, from Octopus, a CSV or your PDF bills.

Export bill

  • {{ sw('green') }}Settled — final. Nothing to do.
  • {{ sw('amber') }}Not settled, under 7 days — wait.
  • {{ sw('red') }}Not settled after 7 days — Re-fetch from Octopus.
  • {{ sw('abandoned') }}No export figure — neither Octopus nor the CSV gave one. A re-fetch may get it; otherwise Fill the missing export from your PDF bills or a CSV.
  • {{ sw('grey') }}Nothing to settle — no export, or CSV history.
  • {{ sw('missing') }}Missing — Fill this gap.

A costed half-hour turning red after a re-fetch

A re-fetch writes only what Octopus's new answer changes. A half-hour Octopus does not answer is left as it was, so a re-fetch never takes away a cost. If a half-hour that was green turns red after one, Octopus's new answer disagrees with itself — or with the day's VAT — as above. That is outside EMT's control; Octopus may correct it later, and a re-fetch then takes the correction.

The one exception is EMT's: Logs showing the bill is stored, the block did not re-settle from it. Make a support file for that day and report it.
🗂️ Whole history

The jobs for your whole history, in the menu at the top:

  • Import history from Octopus — the half-hours Octopus holds, about two years back.
  • Import a CSV and Import PDF bills — for history from before that, or from another supplier.
  • Recover every gap — ask Octopus for every missing half-hour it can fill.
  • Delete all imported history — the half-hours brought in by an import, after showing how many.

For a range, select it on the heatmap instead.

🗄️ Backup & restore
  • Backup now makes a backup at once; Backup & restore lists them — what made each, when, and its size — with the daily copy.
  • Restore one from the list, or drop a backup (a .zip or a blocks.db) anywhere on the page. EMT checks it before anything is touched, and a backup from the previous version of EMT is brought across.
  • Compact now rebuilds the database file to reclaim the space deleted rows left.
🩺 A support file

One zip to send to whoever is helping you: EMT's log, a summary of your setup and the machine it runs on, and your data for the span you choose. Hide my meter and account details (on by default) turns your MPANs, meter serials, account number and postcode into placeholders. Your Octopus API key is never in it, and nothing is sent anywhere — you download it.
