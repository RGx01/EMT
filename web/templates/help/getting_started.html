⚡ What Energy Meter Tracker does

Energy Meter Tracker (EMT) is a Home Assistant add-on that keeps a record of your electricity a half-hour at a time — the same half-hours your supplier bills you by. For each one it keeps how much you imported and exported, what it cost at the rate you were charged, how much of it went to each device you have told it about (a battery, an EV charger, a heat pump), and, with a postcode, how much carbon it carried.

With your supplier connected, EMT checks every half-hour against your supplier's own figures as they arrive, so the history ends up matching your bill.

🚪 The first time

The first time you open EMT it shows the Welcome page, with two ways in:

  • Start fresh — the setup on Settings asks about your supplier and your meter, then which Home Assistant sensors to read.
  • Restore a backup — bring your history across from a backup, including one from the previous version of EMT.

Until setup is done, the other pages are locked.

🔌 Where your readings come from

The setup chooses one of four ways to feed EMT, from what you tell it about your meter:

  • Local meter (CAD) — a consumer access device (an in-home display that reports to Home Assistant) gives your meter readings. No supplier account needed.
  • Local meter (CAD) + Octopus settlement — the local readings, checked against Octopus's settled half-hours.
  • Octopus settlement — Octopus's half-hours alone, with no local meter.
  • Octopus settlement + Octopus Mini — the same, with an Octopus Home Mini for live readings.

With Octopus connected, EMT receives your meter's settled readings (usually a day or two later), then Octopus's cost for each half-hour, and, on Intelligent Octopus Go, your smart-charge dispatches. Your API key is entered on Settings.

With a local meter alone, EMT's figures are a close approximation from your sensor's readings — a good guide, not your bill. With Octopus connected, the settled figures are the ones you are billed from.
🏠 What it needs from Home Assistant
  • A cumulative import reading (kWh) for the main meter — a total that only goes up — and an export one if you export. Not needed when Octopus is the only source.
  • A rate per kWh, from a sensor or from Octopus.
  • For each device you want split out, its own cumulative kWh sensor.
  • Power sensors (W or kW), optional, for the live gauges on Overview.
  • A UK postcode (the first half, e.g. DE1), optional, for carbon.

The Overview, Charts and Insights pages explain what EMT then shows.
