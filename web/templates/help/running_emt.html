💾 Where your data is
  • Everything is in one database, blocks.db, in /data/energy_meter_tracker/.
  • Backups go to /share/energy_meter_tracker_backup_<site>/backups/ (the site's name in the folder's). The 20 most recent are kept. One is made before every change that cannot be undone, and whenever you ask on Data Management.
  • Once a day, at about 3am, blocks.db is copied to /share/energy_meter_tracker_backup_<site>/, beside the backups.
⚠️ Uninstalling the add-on deletes /data/. Make sure there is a recent backup in /share/ first.
🌐 Opening EMT
  • From Home Assistant's sidebar (Show in sidebar on the add-on's page).
  • Directly, at http://<your HA host>:8098. Change the port in the add-on's Network settings to run a second copy beside it, or clear it to use the sidebar only.

Logs, in EMT's own sidebar, shows what it is doing — and is the first thing to look at when something seems wrong.

🧩 EMT's charts in a dashboard

Two pages are made for a Home Assistant dashboard's webpage card:

http://<host>:8098/lovelace/billing   # the Billing chart
http://<host>:8098/lovelace/heatmap   # the heatmap

EMT adds no sensors to Home Assistant: its figures change as half-hours settle, and a sensor's history cannot.

🐳 Standalone Docker

Without Home Assistant's Supervisor, EMT runs as a container of its own, built from Dockerfile.standalone. It needs your Home Assistant's address and a long-lived access token (from your profile in Home Assistant): 

docker run -d --name emt \
  -e HA_URL=http://<your HA host>:8123 \
  -e HA_TOKEN=<your token> \
  -p 8098:8099 \
  -v /path/to/emt-data:/data/energy_meter_tracker \
  emt

Then open http://<host>:8098. EMT listens on 8099 inside the container and is published on 8098, as the add-on is, so it runs beside v4 (8099) without a clash; change the first number to move it.

⚠️ The volume is essential — without it your data goes with the container. In standalone mode backups are kept inside it, in backup/.
