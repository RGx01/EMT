⚙️ Meter Config

A tab each: Site, Main meter, one for each device, and + Add device. An edit on one tab survives moving to another, and Save Config saves them all; the engine picks the change up within seconds.

  • ↺ Refresh Entities — read Home Assistant's sensors again, after adding one.
  • ⚙ Change Setup — the setup wizard again: your supplier, your meter, where your readings come from.
  • 🕓 Billing History — your billing day, address and supplier changes over time.
🏠 Site and ⚡ Main meter
  • Site — its name (used throughout the charts), the first day of your billing period, the timezone, and the postcode prefix for carbon.
  • Main meter — the import and export read sensors (cumulative kWh from and to the grid), the rate and standing charge sensors, or Octopus's, and a power sensor for the live gauge. Each channel's Advanced section holds its MPAN and tariff, shown on the bill card, and its source.
🔋 Devices

A device is a load EMT splits out of your import: a battery, an EV charger, a heat pump or another.

  • Its read sensor — cumulative kWh, a total that only goes up. A charger or a heat pump reports the energy it used. A battery must report the energy it took from the grid, not all it charged: EMT cannot tell its solar charging apart. If its sensor is caught counting solar, EMT says so at the top of every page and changes nothing.
  • Its power sensors — optional, for the Overview's gauges only: a charger's or a heat pump's power, a battery's state of charge and inverter, solar power. A power sensor can be inverted, or its unit set, where it reports the other way round.
  • History sensors — older sensors the device used before its current one. ▶ Rebuild history rebuilds the device's past from Home Assistant's recorder, with them. Importing your history from Octopus or a CSV does this for a device with no history yet, once the import is done — unless its sensor reads more than the house drew, or far less, which the card then says. The card lists the sensors its history came from. ↻ Replace history is for a sensor that turned out to be wrong: after a backup, it drops what the device's rebuilt history and any sensor it no longer uses recorded, and rebuilds it all from the sensors it uses now. Changing a device's sensor asks why — renamed, a new device, or the wrong sensor, which offers the replace.
  • Its colour and rate line on the charts are set on the Charts page, with every meter's: its ⚙︎ menu, Bar colours.
  • Archive — stop recording it from a date, keeping its history (↩ Unretire brings it back). ✕ Remove deletes it and its history, after a backup.

🚗 EV — with Octopus connected and an EV that smart-charges but no EV device, your car still appears in the charts, from Octopus's bills and dispatches. Its colour is set with the others, in the Charts page's Bar colours; an EV device added later takes it on.

🧭 Choosing a device's sensor

A device's Read Sensor is what EMT splits out of your import, every half-hour, and what it rebuilds the device's past from. What it needs:

  • Energy, in kWh, as a running total — the kind Home Assistant's Energy dashboard takes. Not a power sensor (W or kW): power is only for the Overview's gauges, below it.
  • History in Home Assistant's long-term statistics, or there is nothing to rebuild the past from. Check sensors on the device's card asks Home Assistant: ✓ usable, the dates it has, how much of them, and roughly how many kWh in all.
  • The right thing counted. An EV charger or a heat pump: the energy it used — some of it from solar is fine, EMT takes only what the grid gave. A battery: the energy it took from the grid, not all it charged, since EMT cannot tell its solar charging apart. EMT warns where a battery's sensor looks to be counting solar.
  • A believable size. A device cannot use more than the house drew. Before a rebuild EMT compares the sensor with the house; one reading more, or far less, is held with a warning and written only if you say so. About 1,000 times too much is usually a sensor in Wh, not kWh.
🔧 Fixing a device's sensor

When you change a device's Read Sensor and press Save Config, EMT asks why. Answer for what happened:

  • Home Assistant renamed it (or you replaced a deleted sensor with the same device's new one) — Renamed — keep for history. The old name is kept as a history sensor, so rebuilding reaches back past the change, as long as Home Assistant still holds its statistics.
  • The device itself changed — a new charger, a new battery — A new device. What the old sensor recorded stays; the new one carries on from here.
  • You picked the wrong sensor — It was the wrong sensor, below.

The wrong sensor, step by step

  • 1. On the device's tab, put the right sensor in Read Sensor (Check sensors first if you are not sure). If the wrong one is under History sensors, take it out there with ✕.
  • 2. Save Config, and answer It was the wrong sensor.
  • 3. Once saved, EMT offers to replace the device's history. Say yes — or press ↻ Replace history on the card later.
  • 4. EMT takes a backup, then reads the right sensor's history from Home Assistant first: if that fails, nothing is removed. Then it removes everything the wrong sensor recorded — its rebuilt history and its live half-hours — gives that usage back to the house, and rebuilds the device from the right sensor.
  • 5. The card then lists the sensors the device's history came from: only the right one.

Other fixes

  • Undo a rebuild — 🗑 Remove beside Rebuilt history on the card clears the rebuilt half-hours and gives that usage back to the house. What was recorded live, and your imported history, stay. ▶ Rebuild history can then run again.
  • A device that should not be there at all — ✕ Remove deletes it and all its history, after a backup. To stop recording it but keep its past, Archive it.
🌿 Carbon
  • The postcode prefix, if not given at setup.
  • Carbon equivalence — what a petrol car's mile, and the like, is taken to emit, for comparisons on Insights.
  • Battery and heat pump assumptions — the battery's round-trip efficiency; the heat pump's seasonal COP, for the comparison with a gas boiler.
  • Export carbon offset — how the carbon your export saved is counted: at the grid's average intensity, by default, or at a fixed intensity of your own. It changes every export carbon figure — Usage Stats, Running total, Flow and Insights — and the net with it.
