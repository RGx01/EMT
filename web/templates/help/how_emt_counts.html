🕐 The half-hour

Everything EMT keeps is a half-hour — 09:00 → 09:30 — the period your supplier bills by. From a local meter, each half-hour is the difference in its cumulative reading across the half-hour. A reading rarely falls exactly on the boundary, so EMT works out where the meter stood at it from the readings either side.

If the meter reads 1000.0 kWh at 09:28 and 1000.5 kWh at 09:32, about 0.25 kWh crossed the 09:30 boundary — not none, and not all 0.5.

After a restart, a gap of up to 12 hours is filled from the readings either side of it. A longer one is left missing, and shown on Data Management to be filled.

⚖️ The house and the devices

The main meter says how much came from the grid in a half-hour. Each device's sensor says how much it used. The devices' share of the grid comes out of the main meter's; whatever is left is the house's direct import.

  • The EV first: it is the load the cheap rate is meant for, so it takes the grid it used before anything else does.
  • Then the other devices, sharing what is left in proportion to what each used, when the grid did not cover them all (solar or a battery supplied the rest).
  • A device's use beyond its share of the grid came from elsewhere — solar, the battery — and is not priced.

The total is always the main meter's: the split moves cost between the house and the devices, never onto or off your bill.

⏳ Settlement

With Octopus connected, a half-hour waits for Octopus at up to three stages:

  • kWh — the meter's settled reading, usually a day or two later. It says how much.
  • Bill — Octopus's cost for the half-hour, later still. It says what it cost, and it is final.
  • Split — on the capped Intelligent tariff, Octopus's own split of the half-hour between the car and the house.

Until then a half-hour is priced on EMT's own figures, and the settlement pill counts it. On a tariff whose rates are published in advance, the settled kWh × the rate is already final.

💷 Prices and VAT
  • Each half-hour is priced at the rate of the tariff you were on then — from Octopus, or from your rate sensors without it — and, once it arrives, at Octopus's own cost for it.
  • A half-hour on two rates (a cheap window starting mid-way, say) is priced in its parts.
  • The standing charge is a day's, on that local day.
  • VAT is at the rate in force on the day: Octopus's own figures carry it; where a figure has none, EMT applies the statutory rate for the date.
🚗 Intelligent Octopus Go

On Intelligent Octopus Go, Octopus dispatches your car to charge at the cheap rate, often outside the usual night window. EMT records each dispatch as it happens, and a half-hour in one is priced at the cheap rate.

  • The 6-hour cap — on the capped tariff, the cheap rate covers 6 hours of smart charging a day, counted from noon to noon. Charging past them is at the peak rate.
  • Dispatches are only seen live. Octopus does not give past ones, so a dispatch EMT was not running for is not known. The bill is still right — Octopus's cost is the cost — but the car's share of it can only be EMT's estimate, or Octopus's split where it gives one.
🌿 Carbon

With a postcode, EMT records the grid's carbon intensity in your region for each half-hour, from the Carbon Intensity API: its actual figure where the API has one, its forecast where not. A half-hour's carbon is its import × the intensity; export counts against it at the grid's average intensity. Half-hours from before the postcode was set have none.
